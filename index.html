Human variability as a SOTIF input: a specification model for driver and VRU interactions

A working prototype for the master's thesis "How Human Differences Affect Vehicle Safety". It shows how differences in perception, attention, experience and behaviour can be captured as structured, reusable specification entries that feed hazard analysis for reasonably foreseeable misuse under ISO 21448.

Prototype by Praveen Saki. Independent work for the thesis application, not affiliated with or endorsed by Scania. All scenarios and S/C ratings are illustrative examples to show the method; they are not validated analysis results.

Each entry links a human difference to a SOTIF hazardous event

ISO 21448 considers intended use and reasonably foreseeable misuse together with potentially hazardous system behaviour. Here, indirect misuse means the person's deviation does not itself trigger the function, but combined with a functional insufficiency it creates or worsens a hazardous event (for example by reducing controllability). Direct misuse triggers hazardous behaviour on its own. Variability-triggered entries cover human differences that act as triggering conditions without any misuse, such as a VRU outside the perception system's expected appearance.

Coverage shows where the analysis is still thin

Each cell counts entries per function and human-factor dimension. Dashed cells are gaps: either the dimension is irrelevant for that function, which should be argued and recorded, or it has not been analysed yet. This is one way to support completeness arguments in the SOTIF process.

Thesis plan: five phases, mapped to the deliverables




1. ScopeWeeks 1–3. Literature on SOTIF misuse, human factors taxonomies, HARA practice at Scania. Select 2 driver and 2 VRU functions.


2. AnalyseWeeks 4–8. HARA with focus on indirect misuse, workshops with function and safety teams. Compare current methods (HAZOP-style guidewords, STPA).


3. ModelWeeks 9–13. Refine the metamodel, variability ranges with sources, templates and this tool.


4. EvaluateWeeks 14–17. Questionnaire and interviews with representative users: reusability, information quality, relevance.


5. RecommendWeeks 18–20. Governance, ownership of the human-factor library, integration into SOTIF processes, report.




Open questions I would like to discuss: which functions are most valuable to study, how HARA is currently documented, and whether the model should align with an existing tool or modelling language (for example SysML profiles or a feature-model notation for the variability).





Built by Praveen Saki as an application prototype. Scenario content, variability ranges and S/C ratings are illustrative and would be replaced by sourced data and expert judgement during the thesis. Severity and controllability use the S0–S3 and C0–C3 classes known from ISO 26262 and applied in ISO 21448 hazard evaluation.
